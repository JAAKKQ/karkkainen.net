About

I got my first computer when I was nine. It was an older machine, and my brother and I put Ubuntu on it to make it run more smoothly. That was my first real meeting with Linux. At first I mostly played games. Before long I was building websites, experimenting with software, and learning that even simple projects tend to need dependable infrastructure behind them.

That habit of learning by building never really left. Over time it led to home servers, networks, websites, small software projects, and a broader interest in security. It also gave me a practical respect for trade-offs. A system does not become better just because it becomes more complicated. It becomes better when it does its job clearly, safely, and with as little fuss as possible.

How I work

  • I prefer durable systems over fashionable ones.
  • I care about security, but I do not have much patience for security theater.
  • I like documentation because memory is unreliable and good systems should outlast moods.
  • I try to keep business reality in view. Technology has to serve the work, the budget, and the people using it.

Areas I keep returning to

  • System administration and infrastructure
  • Networking and segmentation
  • Secure remote access and practical cyber security
  • Software tools that solve narrow problems well
  • Learning through real projects rather than only theory

Perspective

My internship period in Hong Kong added something important to the technical side of my work. It reminded me that good IT is not only about tools. It is also about execution, cost, vendor choices, and aligning technical decisions with the needs of the business.
